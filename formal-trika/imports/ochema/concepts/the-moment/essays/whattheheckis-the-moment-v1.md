# What the Heck Is the Moment?

**Rendering of `con:the-moment` core v1.0.0, sober revision v1.1.0 (2026-08-04)** — corrected after the peer-review.

---

## The one-sentence version

**The moment is the state transition — the instant in which a state changes. The mathematics of state-generated dynamics describes this precisely; whether "time itself is redefined" at every transition is a hypothesis, not a theorem.**

---

## The problem

We experience time as a river. But physics has never found the river — and
Bennett's Temporal Gap puts the sharpest version of the problem on the
table: is a conscious state at a point in time, or smeared across time?

- If at a point: what makes it a point? Points have no duration — where
  does the "now" come from?
- If smeared: where does the smear live? The past is gone, the future
  hasn't happened — a smeared state has nothing to smear through.

The naive view — "time is a container, moments are slices" — fails on both
horns. There is no container (background-independence). There are no slices
(slicing requires a pre-existing time). Yet there IS change, there IS the
now.

So: what IS the moment, if not a slice of a river?

## What is established (the sober core)

**The state transition.** In the mathematics of states and flows, a state
ω generates a flow σ_t^ω (A — theorem). When the state changes to ω′, a
different flow σ_t^ω′ is associated (A — theorem; the two are related by
the Connes cocycle). So a transition between states is a transition
between the dynamics associated with each state.

**What follows, carefully:**

- The moment as a *state transition* is a precise, mathematical object
  (A — as mathematics).
- "The withdrawal is the moment where time is redefined" is an
  interpretation of that mathematics (C) — the peer-review's point
  stands: state-dependent dynamics under a fixed meta-law is not a
  "change of law," and the phrase "time itself is redefined" overstates
  what the theorems establish.
- **"Why smearing fails — by theorem" is WITHDRAWN.** The old argument
  (a smeared realization is a finite automaton with trivial modular
  structure) rested on the false finite-dim triviality claim. There is
  no theorem here. What remains: if moments are state transitions, then
  a state at a point vs. smeared is a question about the physics of the
  system — open, not settled.

**The triad, recovered at several levels — graded as interpretation:**
the emergence → persistence → withdrawal structure is found in the
Śaiva tradition, in some physics (Barbour's shape dynamics, as an
interpretation), and in the mathematics of transitions. That these are
the SAME structure is interpretive correspondence (C), per the
programme's governing rule: shared vocabulary is not convergence
without a shared constraint or prediction.

- **Shape Dynamics (Barbour):** time emerges from complexity peaks —
  a real and interesting theory; the mapping onto the triad is the
  programme's interpretation (C), not Barbour's claim.
- **Eternalism** would deny the moment special status; the framework's
  answer — "the moment is where the flow is redefined, special by
  theorem" — is corrected to: "the moment is a state transition;
  whether it is special is a hypothesis (C), not settled by theorem."

## The honest caveat

The mathematics of state-generated dynamics is theorem-grade (A). The
identification of the moment with a state transition is a working
model (C). The triad's cross-domain recovery is interpretation (C).
The claim that transitions "redefine time itself" is a hypothesis. The
programme's value is the precise description of the mathematics and
the honest grading of everything else.
