## The one-sentence version

**Love is the most used word in human language and the least defined. The programme's working hypothesis: love names the coherence of a system with what is outside its boundary — an idea that can be studied as a structural hypothesis, and can be compared, soberly, with what the traditions say.**

---

## The problem

The most used word in human language. The least defined. Every
tradition has centered it; no science could touch it. We say "I
love pizza" and "I love you" with the same word, and we know one of
those is a category error — but we can't say which, or why. The
mystics say love is the whole point. The cynics say it's evolved
bonding with a marketing budget.

The programme does not resolve this. It offers a structural
hypothesis worth testing: that part of what we call love is the
coherence a system achieves with what is beyond its boundary —
and that this is a describable, possibly measurable property of
biological and social processes. Graded: hypothesis (C/D), not
established.

## The structural hypothesis (the sober core)

Three pieces, each graded on its own:

1. **Coherence is a describable property of states.** The mathematics
   of states and their dynamics is real (A — as mathematics). Whether
   "the state's coherence" names something measurable in brains or
   bodies is an open empirical question (C/D), not an established
   quantity.
2. **Boundaries are describable.** Systems have boundaries — in
   mathematics (conditional expectations, established), in biology
   (membrane/electrical boundaries, established), in experience (the
   sense of self/other, established phenomenologically). Whether these
   are THE SAME boundary is interpretive correspondence (C), per the
   governing rule.
3. **"Love = coherence across the boundary" is the programme's
   hypothesis.** It is a candidate unification of a word — not a
   demonstrated identity. The traditions' claims about love are
   studied as claims; they are graded as interpretation unless a
   shared constraint or prediction is found.

## What remains worth studying

- The neuroscience of attachment and love — real, established
  science (B), studied in its own terms.
- The traditions' claims (the Tantrāloka's rasa doctrine, the heart
  symbolism, the "love as the point" claims) — studied as the texts'
  claims, compared with the science, graded as interpretation (C).
- The structural hypothesis (love as boundary-coherence) — a
  candidate that earns attention only if it produces a constraint or
  prediction the science or the texts did not already have.

## The honest caveat

The mathematics is established (A where graded, as mathematics). The
neuroscience of attachment is established (B, in its own terms). The
identity of love with coherence across a boundary is a hypothesis
(C/D). The traditions' testimony is interpretation (C) — rich,
ancient, and graded like everything else. The programme says the
least of the three voices: the texts', the science's, and its own.
