# What the Heck Is God? (in Christianity, stated rigorously)

**Rendering of `con:god` core v1.0.0** — the Polkinghorne-rigorous definition,
mapped onto the thesis's machinery. Not a proof of God; a rigorous statement
of the Christian notion, from its best modern articulator, and what that
structure has to do with Ochema.

---

## The one-sentence version

**God (Christian, stated rigorously) is the self-authenticating ground of
all that is — not an entity among entities, but the source; veiled in
encounter; whose deepest reality is the exchange of love (perichoresis);
whose fundamental act is self-limitation (kenosis) — which is why the
tradition's best physicist-theologian describes God in exactly the
structural terms Ochema formalizes: the fixed point, the veil, the
compatibility condition.**

---

## The problem

"God" is the most overloaded word in human language. Everyone has a
version; almost no one has a definition. The internet atheist attacks a
god that no serious theologian believes in — a bearded referee in the
sky, an intervening cause among causes. The internet believer defends
that same god. Both are arguing about a caricature, and neither side
has done the work of stating what the Christian notion actually IS at
its most rigorous.

Polkinghorne did that work. He is the best modern statement of Christian
metaphysics — a quantum field theorist who became an Anglican priest, who
brought the discipline of particle physics to theology, and who spent his
second career refusing both "quantum hype" and lazy fideism. His account
of God is the rigid definition this essay states — and the remarkable
thing is what happens when you lay it next to the thesis's machinery.

## The core (what Polkinghorne actually says)

### 1. God is not an entity among entities

> "He is not an entity among other entities, concerning whom one can say
> 'Lo, here' or 'Lo, there'... Rather, he is the source of all that is,
> the One omnipresent to every human experience. There is no possibility
> of identifying his presence by contrasting it with the different
> experience of his absence." (Reason and Reality, 1991)

This is the single most important sentence in the whole corpus, because
it dissolves most atheist arguments before they start. The atheist
criticizes God as a *thing* — a cause among causes, an object in the
world that happens to be missing. But the Christian notion, rigorously
stated, is that God is not that kind of thing at all. He cannot be found
by contrast, because contrast is the operation of finite things.

**Ochema reads this as:** the exclusion operation E_i: Ω → (P_i, W_i)
is what makes the subject-pole and the world-pole exist — *as poles*.
God is not a pole of the exclusion; he is its ground. The register's
subject can contrast itself with its world; the ground of the whole
operation cannot be contrasted with anything, because contrast is
what it constitutes.

### 2. Aseity — the self-authenticating ground

> "If there is a God, who possesses aseity (being-in-itself), then he is
> self-authenticating; he does not call for evaluation by any other
> criteria." (Reason and Reality, 1991)

The deepest formal notion in the whole definition. A being that does
not call for evaluation by any other criteria — that is the definition
of a *fixed point*. The thesis's subject index ι_i is the
modular-invariant subalgebra of the bearer: the structure that is
invariant under the bearer's own flow, self-constituted by the state's
own structure. Aseity is the theological name for what the thesis calls
the fixed point — with the scope difference that God's fixed point is
the whole, and the subject's is a pole within it.

### 3. The Veil — God is necessarily veiled

> "God is necessarily veiled in his encounter with finite humanity and
> so it would not be altogether surprising if the veil proved
> impenetrable for many." (Reason and Reality, 1991)

> "His acts will be veiled within the unpredictability of complex
> process. They may be discernible by faith but they will not be
> demonstrable by experiment." (Reason and Reality, 1991)

Now the convergence gets specific. The thesis has a concept literally
called the **Veil**: the compatibility condition — the world-pole of
the exclusion, what the register can sustain. The orchestration law
(the state at threshold determines the outcome) is undetectable *as a
mechanism* precisely because it is the state's own law, not a mechanism
among mechanisms. Polkinghorne says the same about divine action: it is
veiled within complex process — discernible in structure, never
demonstrable as a mechanism.

He even says what the thesis says about the pineal-gland hunt:

> "The search for a modern equivalent of the Cartesian pineal gland
> would be the search for a will-o'-the-wisp; it is condemned to
> failure." (Reason and Reality, 1991)

Two independent statements of one epistemic structure: **what can be
known is the structure, not the mechanism.** The thesis from the side
of the subject; Polkinghorne from the side of the ground.

### 4. Perichoresis — God's deepest reality is relational

> "The one true God is constituted by the exchange of love between three
> divine Persons." (Quantum Physics and Theology, 2007)

> "The three divine Persons are held to interpenetrate each other in the
> mutual exchange of love (a theological idea called 'perichoresis')."
> (QPT, 2007)

> "I believe that the true 'Theory of Everything' is not superstrings,
> as physicists are sometimes moved bombastically to proclaim, but it is
> actually trinitarian theology." (QPT, 2007)

The Trinity is the tradition's strongest statement that **relation is
more fundamental than substance** — that the deepest reality is not a
thing but an exchange. The thesis agrees at every level: its invariants
are relational (the moment-triad is three moments in relation; the
harmonic ladder is relations between levels; love is coherence across
the boundary — con:love). Polkinghorne's Trinity is the harmonic
ladder's qualitative content at the divine scale.

And the exchange-of-love claim connects to the thesis's most
externally-supported result: valence as coherence (Joffily & Coricelli
2013, five derivations). If love is coherence across the boundary, and
the Trinity is the exchange of love, then the Trinity is the strongest
possible statement of the valence claim's scale: coherence, fully
extended, with no boundary to defend.

### 5. Kenosis — the fundamental act is self-limitation

> "Love without power would correspond to a God who was a compassionate
> but impotent spectator... Power without love would [be tyranny]."
> (The Causality Distinction, Kenosis, and A Middle Way)

The Christian notion's deepest act is not creation-as-power but
creation-as-withdrawal: God limits himself so that creation can be
free. Kenosis — self-limitation — is the divine version of what the
thesis calls the fundamental operation: **exclusion**. The exclusion
E_i is what makes the world-pole exist by the ground *not being
everything*. The thesis says determination is selection; Polkinghorne
says love is self-limitation. Same operation, two names, two scales.

## What this is NOT

1. **Not a proof of God.** The thesis's machinery is silent on whether a
   divine ground exists. This essay states: IF the Christian notion is
   true, its rigorous form is Polkinghorne's; and that form converges on
   structures the thesis independently derives — which is why the
   tradition's best articulator speaks the language of fixed points,
   veils, and self-limitation without ever having heard of Ochema.
2. **Not a defense of the resurrection-as-pattern claim.** That claim is
   REFUTED-as-stated: a pattern gives no criterion for which copy is me
   (Swinburne). The thesis answers with the subject-as-fixed-point —
   but that answer concerns the mechanism of continuity, not the
   existence of the ground.
3. **Not a God-of-the-gaps.** The thesis is causally closed. The
   orchestration law fills no gap because it is the state's own law.
   Polkinghorne's "intrinsic gaps" (chaos) are not the thesis's
   structure — the convergence is in the veiling, not in the gaps.

## The honest remainder

The atheist is right that no mechanism for God is found — and the
Christian's rigorous answer is that the search for one is condemned to
failure *by structure*, not by ignorance (the pineal-gland passage).
The programme says the same about the subject pole: the mechanism of
determination is veiled because it is the state's own law. This is an
**interpretive correspondence (C)** — the same epistemic structure
described at two scales, not an established identity.

**What the heck is God, then?** The self-authenticating ground of all
that is, whose fundamental act is self-limitation, whose deepest
reality is the exchange of love, and who is necessarily veiled in
encounter — because contrast is the operation he constitutes, not an
operation he is subject to. The thesis cannot verify him. But it can
say, honestly: **if the ground of the exclusion exists, this is
exactly the structure it must have.**
